Thesis Abstract

Land Surface Temperature (LST) is a critical parameter in Earth's energy budget, underpinning applications in climate monitoring, hydrology, agriculture, urban studies, and evapotranspiration estimation. Despite its importance, operational LST retrieval at high spatial and temporal resolution remains challenging due to two fundamental constraints: (i) an inherent spatial–temporal resolution trade-off across satellite platforms, and (ii) persistent cloud-induced data gaps, especially in monsoon-dominated regions like India. This thesis addresses these constraints through four interlinked research objectives.

Objective I — LST Retrieval & Emissivity

Evaluated VI-based emissivity models (NDVI, EVI, NDWI) and single-channel retrieval algorithms (SMWA, GSCA) for Landsat data across 8 sites in India. The Statistical Mono-Window Algorithm (SMWA) achieved RMSE of 1.70 K, significantly outperforming the operational Landsat Level-2 product (RMSE: 3.52 K). Retrieval algorithm choice is more influential than emissivity model selection.

Objective II — All-Weather LST via Passive Microwave

A Random Forest model integrating AMSR2/AMSR-E passive microwave brightness temperatures (multiple frequencies, vertical polarization), NDVI, DEM, and spatiotemporal predictors reconstructs cloud-obscured LST gaps. Validated against MODIS/VIIRS and in-situ data at 12 sites, the model achieves RMSE of 2.40–4.07 K under cloudy conditions, increasing valid LST observations by up to 67% over MODIS-only data.

Objective III — Spatial Downscaling to 100 m

A novel ML/DL framework using ECOSTRESS and Landsat–Sentinel-2 predictors downscales MODIS LST from 1 km to 100 m. Separate time-specific models (RF, XGBoost, ANN, ResNet18, ResNet50) are trained for each MODIS overpass (Terra/Aqua Day/Night). Daytime RMSE: 1.80–1.97 K; Nighttime RMSE: 1.07–1.67 K; R² of 0.95–0.97. ResNet18 excels for daytime; ANN demonstrates consistent performance across all overpass times.

Objective IV — Evapotranspiration Estimation

MODIS, PMW, and gap-filled all-weather LST are integrated into the SPARSE surface energy balance model and validated at 7 eddy covariance sites. Monthly performance shows ~27% accuracy improvement over MODIS-only products (13.12 mm/month RMSE). Gap-filled products provide 53–56% more valid observations, enabling continuous operational ET monitoring for water resource management and precision agriculture.
